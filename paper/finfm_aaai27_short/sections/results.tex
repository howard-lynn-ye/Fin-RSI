\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{Knowing the call is what helps.}
Pooled over \FixModels{} models and \FixArm{} episodes per arm (Figures~\ref{fig:matrix}
and~\ref{fig:analysis}a), F0 was valid in \FixFZeroValid{} episodes (\FixFZeroPct\%), F1 in
\FixFOneValid{} (\FixFOnePct\%) and F2 in \FixFTwoValid{} (\FixFTwoPct\%), better than F0 in
\FixFTwoMinusFZeroBetter{} models and worse in none. With the interface alone, models mostly wrapped a
rule of their own: \FixFOneUsesSignalPositions{} of \FixFOnePrograms{} programs passed their own
states to \texttt{signal\_positions()}, \FixFOneUsesPositions{} called \texttt{positions()}. Documenting
each rule did not change that: F3 matched F1 (\FixFThreeValid{} valid) and only
\FixFThreeLastCalls{} episodes ended on a library call. Asking the model did. \texttt{route()}
returned the task's rule in \FixRouteRight{} of \FixArm{} F4 episodes, nothing in
\FixRouteNone{} and a wrong rule in \FixRouteWrong{} (Figure~\ref{fig:analysis}b), and F4
was valid in \FixFFourValid{} (\FixFFourPct\%): \FixFFourMinusFTwo{} points from F2,
\FixFFourMinusFZero{} over F0, better in \FixFFourMinusFZeroBetter{} models, worse in none, and
at 0 in both for the other \FixFFourSameAtZero.

\paragraph{The gain is in the larger models.}
The \FixLargeModels{} models of \FixSizeCut{} parameters or more (Figure~\ref{fig:headline})
named the right rule in all \FixLargeRouteRight{} of their F4 episodes and rose from \FixLargeFZeroPct\% in F0 to
\FixLargeFFourPct\% (F2 \FixLargeFTwoPct\%); each did better, \FixLargeFFourByOne{} by one
episode. Told the rule, they still kept a rule of their own in \FixLargeFFourToldOwn{} of
\FixLargeFFourTold{} episodes (Figure~\ref{fig:analysis}b). The \FixSmallModels{} smaller models
never exceeded \FixSmallMaxPct\%: after \FixSmallFFourTold{} right routes, no program ran at
all in \FixSmallFFourToldNoProgram; no wrong route gave a valid episode.

\paragraph{What the checks see.}
Of the programs that ran, \FixFZeroFailedExecutionLag{} of \FixFZeroRan{} in F0 traded on a
close they read and \FixFZeroFailedWarmupRowsZero{} held positions in warm-up rows; in F4,
\FixFFourFailedExecutionLag{} and \FixFFourFailedWarmupRowsZero{} of \FixFFourRan{}
(Figure~\ref{fig:analysis}c). Final programs that passed all six checks but were wrong numbered
\FixFZeroAcceptedWrong, \FixFOneAcceptedWrong, \FixFTwoAcceptedWrong, \FixFThreeAcceptedWrong{}
and \FixFFourAcceptedWrong{} in F0--F4; no property check sees another rule or a one-row delay.
Models rarely fixed what a check flagged: with an attempt left, \FixFlaggedThenValid{} of
\FixFlaggedRetry{} such episodes ended valid (F0 \FixFZeroFlaggedThenValid{} of
\FixFZeroFlaggedRetry, F4 \FixFFourFlaggedThenValid{} of \FixFFourFlaggedRetry).

\paragraph{Feedback and self-improvement without the library.}
Of \FrozenArm{} episodes per arm, \texttt{Repeat} was valid in \FrozenRepeat, \texttt{Check} in
\FrozenCheck{} and \texttt{Explain} in \FrozenExplain{} (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:frozen}). Of \SNoLibEpisodes{} episodes on the core models, \texttt{NoImprove} was valid in \SNoLibNoImprove{} and the
other methods in \SNoLibSelfDebug{} (\texttt{SelfDebug}) to \SNoLibCheckFeedback{}
(\texttt{CheckFeedback}, \texttt{Evolve}); every gain came from the two core models that
\texttt{NoImprove} already solved sometimes.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
We revised the library three times after seeing results on these six held-out tasks (each run is
released); the new checks, the factor reading and \texttt{route()} answer what those runs
showed, and every arm was rerun on the last revision. The tasks therefore no longer test a
library fixed in advance; a frozen library on unseen tasks is the next step. The tasks are three
public position rules on synthetic panels, all among the library's own. No experiment measures
returns. The library study's cluster lacks Landlock: programs could not open sockets or start
processes, but file access was not confined, and a scan of every program found none that
touches files, the network or other processes (\FixScanNotPython{} texts did not parse and
could not run). All models are open-weight, at most 32B, with at most
\FixOutputCap{} generated tokens per reply (\StudyOutputCap{} in the earlier study).
