\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\paragraph{The interface alone is not enough.}
Without the library (F0), \FixFZeroValid{} of \FixArm{} episodes were valid (\FixFZeroPct\%); with
its interface in the prompt (F1), \FixFOneValid{} (\FixFOnePct\%) (Figures~\ref{fig:matrix}
and~\ref{fig:analysis}a). Most F1 programs used the library only to wrap a rule of their own:
\FixFOneUsesSignalPositions{} of \FixFOnePrograms{} passed self-computed signals to
\texttt{signal\_positions()}, and \FixFOneUsesPositions{} called \texttt{positions()}. Stating
every algorithm's rule in the prompt (F3) did not change this: F3 was valid in \FixFThreeValid{}
episodes, and only \FixFThreeLastCalls{} ended on a library call.

\paragraph{Naming the call works.}
When the task named the call (F2), \FixFTwoValid{} episodes were valid (\FixFTwoPct\%), better than
F0 in \FixFTwoMinusFZeroBetter{} models and worse in none. With \texttt{route()} the model finds
the call itself: it named the task's rule in \FixRouteRight{} of \FixArm{} F4 episodes, nothing
in \FixRouteNone{} and another rule in \FixRouteWrong{} (Figures~\ref{fig:matrix}
and~\ref{fig:analysis}b). F4 was valid in \FixFFourValid{} episodes (\FixFFourPct\%),
\FixFFourMinusFTwo{} points from F2 and \FixFFourMinusFZero{} over F0. It beat F0 in
\FixFFourMinusFZeroBetter{} models and lost in none; the other \FixFFourSameAtZero{} were at
zero in both.

\paragraph{Larger models gain the most.}
The \FixLargeModels{} models of \FixSizeCut{} parameters or more named the task's rule in all
\FixLargeRouteRight{} of their F4 episodes and went from \FixLargeFZeroPct\% to
\FixLargeFFourPct\% valid, level with F2 (\FixLargeFTwoPct\%). Every one of them improved,
\FixLargeFFourByOne{} by a single episode, and in every series the larger model gained more than
its smaller relatives (Figure~\ref{fig:headline}). Even when told the right call, they kept a
rule of their own in \FixLargeFFourToldOwn{} of \FixLargeFFourTold{} episodes
(Figure~\ref{fig:analysis}b). The \FixSmallModels{} smaller models never passed
\FixSmallMaxPct\% in any arm, and only \texttt{\FixSmallGainModel} gained more than one episode;
after \FixSmallFFourTold{} right routes, no program ran at all in \FixSmallFFourToldNoProgram,
and no wrong route led to a valid episode.

\paragraph{The checks stop wrong programs, but models rarely repair them.}
Without the library, \FixFZeroFailedExecutionLag{} of the \FixFZeroRan{} programs that ran traded
on the close they read and \FixFZeroFailedWarmupRowsZero{} took positions during warm-up; in F4,
\FixFFourFailedExecutionLag{} and \FixFFourFailedWarmupRowsZero{} of \FixFFourRan{} did
(Figure~\ref{fig:analysis}c). None of these was accepted. Some wrong programs still passed all
six checks (\FixFZeroAcceptedWrong, \FixFOneAcceptedWrong, \FixFTwoAcceptedWrong,
\FixFThreeAcceptedWrong{} and \FixFFourAcceptedWrong{} final programs in F0--F4), because no
property check can tell the task's rule from another one or notice a delay of one row. When a
check flagged a program and an attempt remained, only \FixFlaggedThenValid{} of
\FixFlaggedRetry{} such episodes ended valid (F0 \FixFZeroFlaggedThenValid{} of
\FixFZeroFlaggedRetry, F4 \FixFFourFlaggedThenValid{} of \FixFFourFlaggedRetry).

\paragraph{Feedback and self-improvement without the library.}
The more a failure report said, the more episodes ended valid: of \FrozenArm{} episodes per
arm, \FrozenRepeat{} with \texttt{Repeat}, \FrozenCheck{} with \texttt{Check} and
\FrozenExplain{} with \texttt{Explain} (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:frozen}). Under equal budgets
on the core models, \texttt{NoImprove} was valid in \SNoLibNoImprove{} of \SNoLibEpisodes{} episodes and the other
methods in \SNoLibSelfDebug{} (\texttt{SelfDebug}) to \SNoLibCheckFeedback{}
(\texttt{CheckFeedback}, \texttt{Evolve}); every gain came from the two core models that
\texttt{NoImprove} already solved sometimes.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
We revised the library three times after runs on these six held-out tasks; the added checks,
the reading of the factor convention and \texttt{route()} respond to what those runs showed.
Every arm reported here comes from one run on the final library, and the earlier runs are
released, but the tasks no longer test a library fixed in advance; a frozen library on unseen
tasks is the next step. No arm compares \texttt{route()} with text retrieval over the catalogue.
The tasks are three public position rules on synthetic panels, all in the library's catalogue,
and no experiment measures returns. The models are open-weight, at most 32B, with at most
\FixOutputCap{} generated tokens per reply (\StudyOutputCap{} in the earlier study). The
library study's cluster had no Landlock: programs could not open sockets or start processes, but
file access was not confined; a scan of every program found none that touches files, the
network or other processes (\FixScanNotPython{} texts did not parse and could not run).
