\paragraph{The library helps the models that call it.}
Pooled over \FixModels{} models and \FixArm{} episodes per arm, F0 was valid in
\FixFZeroValid{} episodes (\FixFZeroPct\%), F1 in \FixFOneValid{} (\FixFOnePct\%) and F2 in
\FixFTwoValid{} (\FixFTwoPct\%). F2 did better than F0 in \FixFTwoMinusFZeroBetter{} models and
worse in \FixFTwoMinusFZeroWorse{} (\FixFTwoMinusFZero{} points pooled); F1 did better than F0 in
\FixFOneMinusFZeroBetter{} and worse in \FixFOneMinusFZeroWorse{} (\FixFOneMinusFZero{} points).
With the interface alone (F1), models imported \texttt{fin\_skills} in
\FixFOneUsesImportsFinSkills{} of \FixFOnePrograms{} programs, mostly to wrap a rule they wrote
themselves: \FixFOneUsesSignalPositions{} programs passed their own states to
\texttt{signal\_positions()}, which lags, fills and scales them, and \FixFOneUsesPositions{}
called \texttt{positions()}. Most of F1's valid episodes came from that wrapping
(\FixFOneLastUsesSignalPositionsValid{} of \FixFOneValid). One sentence naming the call (F2)
moved models to \texttt{positions()}: \FixFTwoUsesPositions{} programs called it, and in
\FixFTwoLastCalls{} episodes the last program called \texttt{positions()} or \texttt{run()}.
\FixFTwoLastCallsChecked{} of those ran and passed the checks, and \FixFTwoLastCallsValid{} were
valid, against \FixFTwoLastOwnValid{} of the \FixFTwoLastOwn{} episodes whose last program did
not call them.

\paragraph{What still goes wrong.}
In F2, \FixFTwoLastCallsAcceptedWrong{} programs that called the library and passed the checks
were wrong; \FixFTwoLastCallsRescaled{} of them divided its result, which is already scaled, by
the number of assets again, which the interface text warns against and no check can see. Of the
\FixLibErrors{} library calls in F1 and F2 that raised, the commonest errors were
\FixLibErrorOne{} (\FixLibErrorOneCount), \FixLibErrorTwo{} (\FixLibErrorTwoCount) and
\FixLibErrorThree{} (\FixLibErrorThreeCount). Most errors name the call to make, but small models
rarely recovered within three programs: \FixFTwoNoneValid{} models had no valid F2 episode.

\paragraph{Feedback and self-improvement without the library.}
In the study fixed on 8~November, out of \FrozenArm{} episodes per arm, \texttt{Repeat} was
valid in \FrozenRepeat, \texttt{Check} in \FrozenCheck{} and \texttt{Explain} in
\FrozenExplain: \texttt{Check} minus \texttt{Repeat} \FrozenCheckRepeat{} points and
\texttt{Explain} minus \texttt{Check} \FrozenExplainCheck. Out of \SNoLibEpisodes{} episodes on
the \SNoLibModels{} core models, \texttt{NoImprove} was valid in \SNoLibNoImprove,
\texttt{CheckFeedback} and \texttt{Evolve} in \SNoLibCheckFeedback{} and \SNoLibEvolve,
\texttt{PlaybookLearning} in \SNoLibPlaybook, \texttt{Reflexion} in \SNoLibReflexion,
\texttt{Team} in \SNoLibTeam{} and \texttt{SelfDebug} in \SNoLibSelfDebug.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The study covers three position rules on synthetic panels, all among the library's own; the
newer tasks and the real panel are released but not evaluated here. All tasks are public, so a
model may have seen them. No experiment measures returns, and we make no claim about them. The
checks reject only the failures they were written for. The library study ran on a cluster whose
kernel lacks Landlock: programs could not open sockets or start processes, but file access was
not confined, so we scanned every program afterwards (\FixScanFlagged{} mention file or process
access; Appendix). Its refusals of silent mistakes were added after a first run of the same
design, whose records we release; F0 never imports the library and is taken from that run. All
models are open-weight, with at most \FixOutputCap{} generated tokens per reply in the library
study and \StudyOutputCap{} in the earlier one, which cuts off long reasoning.
