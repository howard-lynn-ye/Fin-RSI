One episode: a one-line bug that runs without error, caught by a check and confirmed by a grader the method
never sees. (a)~Amazon's 20-for-1 split on 6 June 2022 (real closes, indexed): raw quotes fall
\EpisodeDrop\% in one day. Run~1 of (b) reads the raw quotes, so its momentum signal is short Amazon
for the \EpisodeShort{} sessions after the split; on \EpisodeWrong{} of them (shaded) the task's
independent reference is long. (b)~On task \texttt{momentum\_a} the judge runs three checks.
Run~1 fails \texttt{split\_representation}; the method gets the failed check, its cause (shortened
here) and the \texttt{split\_once} rule card, never the grade, and retries within its budget of
\BudgetAttempts{} runs. Run~2 changes that one line and passes. Only then, behind the wall, does
the hidden grader compare the final program with the reference; its verdict goes to the
benchmark alone. These are the judge's real verdicts on every synthetic seed and on the real
panel. (c)~The third check on a synthetic panel: the library's lagged inverse-volatility
positions first move at row $k{+}1$ and pass; read one row early, they move at row $k$ and
fail.}
