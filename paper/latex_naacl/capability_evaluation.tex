\paragraph{E1: detecting research defects.}
The benchmark plants 12 defect families, including temporal leakage, unadjusted corporate
actions, and universe-selection errors, in three synthetic worlds. The suite records
\CaughtInstances{}/\DefectInstances{} detections and no false alarms on its clean
references. This is suite-level recall: a defect may be caught by another applicable
guard even when one intended check misses it. Repeating a family across seeds does not
create a new defect type. Appendix~\ref{app:audit} describes the independent oracle and
the distinction between diagnosis and agent repair.

\paragraph{E2: execution and audit compliance.}
We count actual dispatches and final artifacts rather than statements that a check ran.
The exploratory portfolio study completed all 64 decisions, including 16 with autonomous
tool access, and logged 43 successful requests in that arm. These requests include
catalog lookup and guidance retrieval as well as numerical algorithms. In the separate
audit feasibility study, optional guards and mandatory enforcement have different
acceptance outcomes. Mandatory rejection protects the submission boundary but does not
constitute successful repair: the 14B model reached its turn limit in all three mandatory
cells. The reference submission passed the public checks and independent grading.

\paragraph{E3: numerical parity.}
Eight checks compare equal weighting, inverse-volatility weighting, minimum-variance
optimization, normal and historical VaR/ES, historical volatility, fractional-difference
weights, and an augmented Dickey--Fuller statistic with closed forms or independent
NumPy, SciPy, pandas, and statsmodels references. All meet their recorded tolerances;
the largest absolute difference is $1.18\times10^{-8}$ for the optimization weights.
The optimization case allows $10^{-3}$ while other cases have their own tighter
tolerances. This supports agreement for the tested calculations, not exact equality
or validation of all catalog entries.

\paragraph{Auxiliary integrations.}
The context and memory studies test optional consumers of the library's results.
Their methods and outcomes appear in the appendices. The public FinQA RAG and reranking
runs retain their original official scores (\FQLegacyTotal/\FQAnswers{} correct programs
across six model and retrieval cells). A post-hoc reanalysis bound its target-blind
extractions by hash before grading: accepting a single fenced answer adds no correct
program, and counting explicit correct final numbers raises the total to
\FQRecoverableTotal/\FQAnswers. That second count is not program accuracy. Of the
\FQMistralAnswers{} Mistral answers, \FQMistralMissingTotal{} have no final answer, so
format repair alone cannot isolate retrieval quality (Appendix~\ref{app:library}).
Interface corrections require a new versioned comparison rather than retrospective
replacement of the recorded grades.
